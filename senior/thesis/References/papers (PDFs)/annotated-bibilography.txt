Papers supporting Scalar Equilibrium Matching for Predicting Protein Evolvability
Selected 17 October 2026 against documentation/Thesis Proposal/proposal.md

Ten additional papers; the existing AI Redesigned Protein Evolution.pdf is preserved.
Q1 = useful scalar landscapes versus supervised/implicit alternatives.
Q2 = sequence-to-measured-function relationship.
Q3 = predicting a starting sequence's evolvability from its neighborhood.

WHY THESE PAPERS FORM A CHAIN
Statistical sequence energies can capture mutation effects, and pretrained embeddings
already enable strong supervised predictors. EqM supplies the methodological starting
point, but its explicit-energy experiments leave an optimization/performance gap.
Energy Matching offers a relevant alternative scalar formulation, while discrete MCMC
shows how differentiable scores can guide actual amino-acid mutations. Biological
experiments then show why current activity alone does not identify the best starting
sequence: stability, permissive substitutions, and epistasis change accessible outcomes.
That combination motivates testing whether a learned landscape's neighborhood adds
predictive value beyond the starting sequence's predicted function.
This is a synthesis motivating your existing questions, not evidence they are solved.

SUGGESTED READING ORDER
Bloom -> Baier -> Wu -> Starr -> Hopf -> Alley -> ProteinGym -> EqM ->
Energy Matching -> Plug & Play Directed Evolution. Then revisit your existing
AI-redesigned starting-points paper as the modern experimental endpoint.
The numbered entries below follow this order.

1. Bloom et al. (2006). Protein stability promotes evolvability. PNAS.
File: Bloom_2006_Protein_Stability_Promotes_Evolvability.pdf
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0510098103
PDF: https://cheme.caltech.edu/groups/fha/publications/Bloom_PNAS2006.pdf
Questions: Q3.
Why: Controlled cytochrome P450 experiments connect extra stability to tolerance of
mutations and acquisition of new/improved activities. This provides a mechanistic
reason to examine the fraction of functional or improving mutants around a starting
sequence rather than its current activity alone.
Read for: experimental mutant yields and the stability/robustness explanation.
Limit: thermodynamic stability and your model's scalar energy are different quantities;
the paper does not establish that any learned energy automatically measures stability.

2. Baier et al. (2019). Cryptic genetic variation shapes the adaptive evolutionary
potential of enzymes. eLife 8:e40789.
File: Baier_2019_Cryptic_Genetic_Variation.pdf
DOI: https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.40789
PDF: https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/bitstreams/418474bc-0ae1-4ecd-ba4a-5d96b2a0e4eb/download
Questions: Q3; strongest direct comparison of alternative starting sequences here.
Why: Four orthologous metallo-beta-lactamases follow different directed-evolution
outcomes. A low-initial-fitness starting protein reaches a higher plateau, while the
highest-initial-activity protein reaches an inferior outcome. Background-dependent
mutation effects make evolvability distinct from present-day function.
Read for: comparative trajectories, first adaptive mutations, and epistasis.
Limit: a specific enzyme system; neighborhood statistics must be tested against
outcomes rather than assumed to generalize.

3. Wu et al. (2016). Adaptation in protein fitness landscapes is facilitated by
indirect paths. eLife 5:e16965.
File: Wu_2016_Adaptation_via_Indirect_Paths.pdf
DOI: https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.16965
PDF: https://cdn.elifesciences.org/articles/16965/elife-16965-v4.pdf
Questions: Q3 and the neighborhood-evaluation data requirement.
Why: A combinatorial GB1 landscape over four sites exposes blocked direct adaptive
paths and indirect routes that restore accessibility. It makes landscape topology
and epistasis concrete, rather than treating a fitness landscape as a metaphor.
Read for: reciprocal sign epistasis and indirect paths through added/lost mutations.
Limit: 160,000 is the full four-site combinatorial space, not a full-protein map.
A favorable single-mutation neighborhood need not determine long-term evolvability.

4. Starr, Picton & Thornton (2017). Alternative evolutionary histories in the
sequence space of an ancient protein. Nature.
File: Starr_2017_Alternative_Evolutionary_Histories.pdf
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/nature23902
PDF: https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/voices.uchicago.edu/dist/7/477/files/2017/09/Starr-Thornton_Nature2017-txzy25.pdf
Questions: Q3.
Why: Ancestral reconstruction and deep mutational scanning show that permissive
background substitutions enable alternative functional outcomes. It explains why
sequence backgrounds with similar ancestral function can have different accessible
futures, complementing Baier's comparative laboratory evolution.
Read for: permissive substitutions and networks of functional sequences.
Limit: historical receptor-function evolution is not the same endpoint as a modern
directed-evolution campaign; this is mechanistic support, not direct validation.

5. Hopf et al. (2017). Mutation effects predicted from sequence co-variation.
Nature Biotechnology 35:128-135 (EVmutation).
File: Hopf_2017_Mutation_Effects_from_Sequence_Covariation.pdf
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/nbt.3769
PDF: https://public.websites.umich.edu/~zhanglab/clubPaper/02_22_2017.pdf
Questions: Q1/Q2, with a bridge to Q3 through context-dependent mutations.
Why: A pairwise statistical sequence model assigns scalar scores and predicts
mutation effects from evolutionary covariation. This is a concrete precedent for
useful sequence energies and a comparator for the claim that energy captures
interactions beyond an ordinary independent-site model.
Read for: epistatic versus independent models and experimental mutation-effect tests.
Limit: unsupervised evolutionary probability is not assay-specific fitness or physical
folding free energy. Its positive delta-E denotes increased probability; align signs
before comparing with your proposal's lower-energy-is-better convention.

6. Alley et al. (2019). Unified rational protein engineering with sequence-based
deep representation learning. Nature Methods 16:1315-1322 (UniRep).
File: Alley_2019_Unified_Rational_Protein_Engineering_UniRep.pdf
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-019-0598-1
PDF: https://ccsp.hms.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Unified-rational-protein-engineering-with-sequence-based-deep-representation-learning-Alley-Church.pdf
Questions: Q1/Q2; secondary bridge to Q3.
Why: Pretrained sequence representations support simple supervised models of
protein properties. This directly motivates your embedding-based baseline: the
energy formulation must add value beyond a representation plus ordinary predictor.
Read for: low-data generalization and Figure 4's predicted brightness under mutation.
Limit: predicted mutational robustness is not independent experimental proof of
starting-sequence evolvability. Download is an author manuscript.

7. Notin et al. (2023). ProteinGym: Large-Scale Benchmarks for Protein Fitness
Prediction and Design. NeurIPS, Datasets and Benchmarks.
File: Notin_2023_ProteinGym.pdf
Paper: https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/cac723e5ff29f65e3fcbb0739ae91bee-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.html
PDF: https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/cac723e5ff29f65e3fcbb0739ae91bee-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.pdf
Questions: Q1/Q2 and sequence-function data/evaluation.
Why: A common benchmark makes it possible to evaluate sequence-based fitness
prediction across assays and model families rather than choosing one favorable
example. It grounds the proposal's comparison with existing approaches and its
need for measured sequence-function datasets.
Read for: supervised versus zero-shot settings, splits, and evaluation metrics.
Limit: success on mutation-effect prediction does not itself establish prospective
multi-round evolvability. Assay readouts are not interchangeable biological fitness.

8. Wang & Du (2025). Equilibrium Matching: Generative Modeling with Implicit
Energy-Based Models. arXiv:2510.02300.
File: Wang_Du_2025_Equilibrium_Matching.pdf
Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02300
PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.02300
Questions: Q1; the methodological foundation explicitly cited in your proposal.
Why: Defines the equilibrium-gradient objective and optimization-based sampling.
Section 3.2 gives explicit-energy EqM-E constructions; Section 5.3/Table 5 reports
degraded generation performance and initialization sensitivity for the L2 variant.
This is the precise empirical motivation for investigating your scalar formulation.
Read for: Sections 3.1-3.3 and the explicit-energy ablation, not only the abstract.
Limit: existing EqM-E already learns a scalar potential. Your contribution therefore
needs comparison with those variants. Image generation performance does not show
that energy ranks protein function; that relationship needs its own training/evidence.

9. Balcerak et al. (2025). Energy Matching: Unifying Flow Matching and Energy-Based
Models for Generative Modeling. NeurIPS 2025; arXiv:2504.10612.
File: Balcerak_2025_Energy_Matching.pdf
Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10612
PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.10612
Questions: Q1; methodological alternative supporting the scalar-energy comparison.
Why: Learns a single time-independent scalar field with transport behavior away
from the data manifold and equilibrium behavior nearby. It provides a particularly
close existing approach for assessing what a direct scalar formulation contributes,
and includes a controlled protein generation demonstration.
Read for: scalar objective, near-manifold behavior, and protein generation example.
Limit: Energy Matching and Equilibrium Matching are different methods. A protein
generation demonstration is not evidence of measured sequence-function prediction
or starting-sequence evolvability.

10. Emami et al. (2023). Plug & Play Directed Evolution of Proteins with
Gradient-based Discrete MCMC. Machine Learning: Science and Technology.
File: Emami_2023_Plug_and_Play_Directed_Evolution.pdf
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1088/2632-2153/accacd
Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09925
PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.09925
Questions: Q1/Q3 and the Method section's continuous-to-discrete difficulty.
Why: Combines unsupervised evolutionary models and supervised function predictors,
then uses gradients to propose mutations directly in discrete protein space.
This supplies an existing route from differentiable scores to sequences and a
comparator for whether Scalar EqM offers useful additional landscape structure.
Read for: product-of-experts formulation and gradient-based mutation proposals.
Limit: reported evolution experiments are in silico and use predicted function;
they do not independently validate biological evolvability or decode arbitrary
continuous embedding points into viable sequences.

SELECTION AND VERIFICATION
Sources are publisher, arXiv, or institutional/author-hosted PDFs. All ten new files
were opened with PDF tools, checked for extractable full-paper text and matching
titles, and checked for distinct content. Some downloads are author manuscripts
or preprints rather than publisher typeset versions. This guide is a focused
selection and relevance assessment, not an exhaustive literature review or a
reproduction of the papers' experimental results. The proposal was not edited.

FOLLOW-UP: WHY PARAMETERIZE A SCALAR ENERGY? (17 October 2026)
Du et al. (2023), Reduce, Reuse, Recycle: Compositional Generation with
Energy-Based Diffusion Models and MCMC. ICML.
File: Du_2023_Reduce_Reuse_Recycle_Energy_Diffusion.pdf
PDF: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/du23a/du23a.pdf
Read Section 4.2 and Appendix E (PDF pages 5-6 and 18-19).
Appendix E describes scalar-output networks trained through their input gradients.
Explicit potentials enable energy-dependent composition and Metropolis correction.
Caveat: its selected architecture transforms a vector output into an energy; it
does not establish that an ordinary scalar-output head improves EqM performance.
For the exact EqM loss, see EqM Section 3.2, equation 7.
The Slack exchange proposes gradient-target training, not scalar-target regression.
