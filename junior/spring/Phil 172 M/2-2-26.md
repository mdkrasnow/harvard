## Singer's Principle

If you can prevent something bad from happening without sacrificing anything of comprable moral worth, you should.

This implies a few things: 
- The MANY matter
- Fit the FEW into the broader moral picture that STARTS with the many


## What is a Project?

> A project is a set of actions that are taken to achieve a goal. 

Our GROUND PROJECTS are FUNDAMENTAL to our sense of self. They define our sense of worth and purpose.
- Marriage, children, career, health, etc. 

## Williams #1


Williams is troubled by this because he thinks that Singer's Principle violates the value of our ground projects.

> Why? Well, it leaves no place for the value of your own personal living. Do you need to give up your ground projects in some cases. What if you could benefit more people by being a doctor instead of a philosophy professor, which is your passion? 


Williams thinks that a good moral framework should not ask you to give up your ground projects. 

### Argument (Syllogism)

premise 1) Singers principal can require us to give up our ground projects

premise 2) Ground Projects are morally valuable

conclusion) We are NOT morally required to follow SP

> **Remark:** If chosing between killing your spouse and killing someone random, I think that there is an obligation to kill the random person because if you provide this option for everyone on Earth, you will likely be killed in most situations. We cannot have people killing people who make an important contribution to their own lives, otherwise society would not exist. The purpose of morals is to prolong the existence of the many, however intermediary goals may not directly contribute to this. Having a stable society is KEY for prolonging the existence of the many. Societies must be built on trust of each other and the people that you are close to. Even though providing for people that you care about more than others may not directly contribute to prolonging the existence of the many, it is still morally valuable.
